HOW IT WORKS

Three layers of proof

In your browser. A file is split into 256 KB chunks. Each chunk is sealed with its own key, derived so that no two chunks ever share one, and bound to its position — so a chunk cannot be reordered, duplicated or swapped in from another file without decryption failing outright.

In storage. Only ciphertext is uploaded, and each chunk is addressed and fetched independently. The storage provider learns how large a file is and when it arrived. Nothing more.

On the ledger. A Merkle root committing to every chunk is anchored publicly. Any single chunk can later be proven to belong to the registered document — without downloading, decrypting or disclosing the rest of it.

ANCHORING

One transaction, thousands of documents

Registering documents one at a time means one transaction each — a cost that grows with every upload, and a wallet every user has to own. Neither scales to a platform.

Instead, a signed receipt is issued the instant your file is stored. Documents are then collected and committed together through a single Merkle root, published in one transaction. Each document keeps its own independent inclusion proof, a few hundred bytes regardless of how many it was batched with.

A batch of ten thousand costs what a batch of one costs. The price per document falls as the platform grows, rather than rising with it.

Your browser checks both halves itself. The receipt's signature is verified against the key that signed it, and once the batch is anchored the inclusion proof is checked against a batch root read from the chain — never against the root the service supplied with it. A service that receipted one document and anchored another is caught by that comparison, in your tab.

Registering from your own wallet is still offered on the upload page, for anyone who wants no party at all between them and the chain.
